==============================================================================
HOW OFTEN DOES THE SAME COMMAND GET A DIFFERENT ANSWER?
==============================================================================

Identical bytes, repeated calls. The question is not 'what is the flip rate'
-- there is no single flip rate -- but 'where in the score range do flips
live, and how much of a real workload lands there'.

  What 'within 0.05 of tau' is and is not: FINDINGS 2.3 saw flips within
  0.05 of tau=0.50, on items whose probability was genuinely uncertain. It
  also measured sd 0.000 at p=0.97 -- Jev does not wobble when it is
  confident. Distance from tau and intrinsic uncertainty coincide at
  tau=0.50 and come apart at tau=0.95, where a crowded band may be crowded
  with items that are perfectly stable. Read occupancy as an EXPOSURE
  UPPER BOUND, not as a flip count; only the sweep separates the two, and
  when it lands the flip-rate-versus-distance table must be read per tau,
  never pooled across them.

  The synthetic set is balanced 1:1:1 by design, so its occupancy figure is
  an artefact of how it was written, not a workload. The live base rate is
  far more skewed and the live occupancy number is the one that decides
  whether enforcement is safe. It needs live captures, not more synthetic
  ones.
